# Why this comparison is worth running

The question is whether same-original cash-flow support and the preceding price response improve five-session event selection. The five inputs provide a specific mechanism: seasonal earnings change (`S`), cash-backing change (`Q`), recent market-relative price movement, unusual dollar activity, and broad market trend. A regularized linear model tests additive information; the fixed boosted trees test interactions such as earnings strength behaving differently after a large price move. Both run regardless of whether a simple control makes money. This is a bounded comparison, not a claim that complexity itself creates an edge.

All five recipes use the same causal, complete-feature event population. The all-filing and `S>=1` controls measure whether adding `Q` and the other features changes selection; `S>=1 and Q>0` isolates the simplest cash-support hypothesis. Missing financial facts stay unavailable rather than entering a model as an imputed zero. The initial six-issuer pilot is too small to train this comparison, which is why the 100-issuer expansion, selected without using returns, precedes fitting.

Every candidate event also needs an original header SIC in 0100–5999 or 7000–8999 from an unambiguous FILER block linked to that exact CIK. This prevents current classification from admitting a financial-business event with different cash-flow semantics. Financial or other out-of-range codes are excluded; missing, conflicting or malformed codes and uncertain filer linkage remain unavailable. All five recipes share the gate, with no current-SIC fallback or issuer replacement. The gate applies to the candidate event: the frozen eight-event S history is preserved, so prior-industry changes remain a limitation. Current incorporation and common-share title still do not establish historical domicile or security continuity.

The model presets are reused exactly: Ridge `alpha=100`; HGB with 60 iterations, 15 maximum leaves, learning rate 0.05, L2=10, minimum 64 rows per leaf, no early stopping, and seed 1729. They have not been chosen from the new cohort's returns. With only 256 fitting events, the 64-row leaf minimum permits at most four leaves in a tree, limiting interactions when evidence is thin. Ridge's penalty is applied after fitting-only scaling. Neither model has a family-selection contest, threshold search, alternative horizon, outcome-driven feature choice or fallback family. Their two outputs are return and adverse close movement; they are not attention heads or a shared neural representation.

The minimum 256 fitting rows across 25 CIKs and 64 calibration rows across 16 CIKs are pragmatic floors, not a statistical proof of sufficient independent observations. They prevent a handful of repeated issuer quarters from supporting a nominally broad model. Calibration estimates just two offsets; nevertheless, earnings dates cluster and observations overlap, so apparent sample size can overstate information. A fold failing either floor abstains. The thresholds will not be reduced after discovering missing facts or inactive strategies.

Expanding annual folds keep all 2021–2025 test decisions later than fitting and calibration. Outcomes crossing the next partition are purged. Every family/year produces one forecast map shared by USD10,000 and USD2,000 accounts under both costs. This yields 10 scheduled model fits and 20 continuous account paths. Different starting cash changes whole-share affordability, fee burden and sizing, so capital is economically meaningful; it does not change the source cohort or model training.

Mean-residual calibration is deliberately the existing convention: clip uncalibrated downside at zero, estimate each head's mean error on prior calibration rows, apply its offset, then clip corrected downside at zero. Return forecasts stay unbounded, with impossible sale projections rejected by the planner. This corrects average bias but does not repair overly optimistic tails or ranking. Earlier calibration concerns therefore remain relevant: retain raw/corrected predictions and report future-bin performance. Return-slope shrinkage or a different calibration model would be a separate declared comparison, not an undocumented favorable substitution in this one.

One implementation boundary matters: `_fit_family` accepts two target columns, but the existing validation/prediction wrappers expect six. Reuse the fitter and its preprocessing, then validate direct two-column model predictions in the isolated adapter. Do not duplicate horizons or silently change shared six-head code to make shapes fit.

The same planner enforces whole shares, settled cash, five slots, 20% position caps and 0.5% NAV risk sizing over a two-ATR distance. Learned entries additionally require positive expected net-dollar utility after the declared costs and 0.5 downside penalty. Preserve original reservations and allow only fill-time quantity reductions. Closing prices and adjusted-notional corporate-action handling remain simulated conventions; minimum broker fees can still overwhelm a forecast's expected dollars.

Report every calendar week of the full account, including idle and losing weeks. Preserve the raw weekly returns and calculate the exact fractions reaching 5%, 10% and 15%, median and worst week, and longest consecutive losing-week streak. Label initialization and liquidation-tail weeks separately. Annualized returns or trade averages cannot establish a weekly target-hit rate.

The owner's 5% weekly aspiration and preference for greater returns do not become fabricated predictions, permissive thresholds or a reason to exceed the 30% drawdown limit. The research must report inactivity and losses honestly. A favorable historical result would still need sufficient issuer/year support and source/execution qualification before any separate promotion decision. The present stock study does not silently become an options or ETF experiment when the wider project mandate expands.
