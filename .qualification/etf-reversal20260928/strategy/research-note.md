# Can short ETF dislocations pay after costs?

The experiment buys an equity ETF after a large two-session fall relative to its usual relationship with SPY. A temporary selling imbalance could reverse; a lasting change in sector prospects may continue. The test distinguishes those possibilities without assuming that every sharp decline is cheap.

For each of ten fixed broad-market/sector ETFs, we estimate its market sensitivity from sixty earlier daily returns, excluding the two days used to measure the dislocation. We buy only when the resulting residual score is at or below -2, with the most negative scores considered first. One separately declared version also requires SPY to exceed its 200-session average. This tests whether avoiding persistent market declines helps, while accepting that the filter may miss recoveries. There is no threshold search.

The idea draws on research into residual reversals, but it is a different implementation. Published individual-stock, long/short evidence does not establish an edge in these long-only ETFs. Removing SPY from a signal also does not remove market exposure from the purchased position. [Avellaneda and Lee](https://math.nyu.edu/~avellane/AvellanedaLeeStatArb20090616.pdf), [Da, Liu and Schaumburg](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/Reversal.pdf).

Signals become available after the daily close and enter at the following close. Positions exit three sessions after entry. This lets us test short corrections with the existing cash engine and avoids pretending that a completed closing-price signal could trade at that same close. The delay may consume the reversal; losses under this timing would be evidence against this executable version. We use whole shares, settled cash, at most five positions and the same predetermined position/risk limits. A sampled-close 30% drawdown triggers permanent suspension of entries and next-close liquidation; gaps can still overshoot that level.

The control has the same signal dates and candidate counts as the unfiltered reversal rule, but chooses ETFs with a fixed date/symbol hash order. It answers whether selecting the residual losers helps beyond simply taking ETF exposure on those dates. It can overlap the selected ETFs, and affordability, holdings and volatility can create different executed exposure. Reduced losses from holding more cash do not establish better stock selection. Flat cash is also reported.

All twelve accounts will be retained: three policies, USD10,000 and USD2,000, and Wealthsimple-USD/Moomoo cost profiles. A uniform additional friction allowance is determined independently from the predeclared ETF quote sample, then frozen before account results. Quoted spreads are not actual fills. Taxes, CAD/USD conversion and incomplete corporate-action cash treatment remain outside this simulation.

Support requires net gains beyond cash and useful, reasonably stable improvement over the control. A result concentrated in one ETF or brief episode, excessive drawdown, or an advantage erased by relevant costs would not justify promotion. We will report every year and calendar week, including idle and losing weeks, full-account fees/exposure, worst week and losing streaks. The owner's 5%, 10% and 15% weekly aspirations will be measured on the entire account after modeled costs, with actual hit fractions rather than inferred promises.

The source panel and causal signals are prepared; no ETF account result is asserted here. These are currently listed instruments with revised historical prices, not a historical all-ETF universe or an untouched holdout. The next concrete step is the fixed twelve-account comparison after independent source/signal and cost checks.
