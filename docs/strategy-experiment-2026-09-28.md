## Exact formulas

Let P_i,t be the frozen adjusted price and B_t the SPY adjusted price. Let r_i,t = log(P_i,t/P_i,t-1), b_t = log(B_t/B_t-1), L_i,k,t = sum over the last k sessions of (r_i-b), and s_i,t the sample standard deviation of the previous 20 residual returns (r_i-b), ending t-1. A_i,t is mean dollar activity over t-4 through t divided by mean over t-24 through t-5. RV_k is the annualized sample standard deviation of the last k simple returns. All calculations preserve missing values; absent prerequisites mean no score. Score arrays use float32, matching the existing relative-rule output, with intermediate beta and benchmark calculations in float64. Alphabetical symbol order breaks score ties through a stable descending sort.

## Hypotheses and refutations

1. **Market-neutralized short reversal:** replacing the beta=1 market subtraction removes broad-market moves misclassified as stock-specific weakness. Refute a useful improvement if the paired net-equity difference versus original reversal2 is nonpositive, or if apparent improvement is concentrated in one year or source-action tail. A smaller loss alone is not profitable alpha.
2. **Pullback within a continuing relative trend:** a five-session relative decline can be a retracement when both long stock trend and medium relative trend remain positive. Refute if the gated rule loses more net equity than relative reversal5, or merely reduces exposure without improving net expectancy and weekly downside. Retain inactivity as a result.
3. **Continuation with compression:** high activity accompanied by positive medium relative trend and decreasing short volatility may distinguish orderly continuation from unstable jumps. Refute if net expectancy/paired equity fail to improve over existing volume continuation, or the gate leaves too little activity for useful inference. No relaxation to force trades.
4. **Regime-dependent mixture:** reversal and continuation may be useful in different observable conditions. Compare the fixed equal-rank mixture with each constituent, then the causal selector with that fixed mixture. Refute added timing value if selector's paired equity difference is nonpositive or an apparent drawdown benefit is explained solely by more cash while net expectancy deteriorates. Report all three holding horizons rather than selecting a best-period oracle.
5. **Holding duration matters:** 1/3/5-session outcomes assess how much next-close delay and turnover consume each tactic's return. This is a descriptive three-way comparison with all paths shown, not selection of an optimal horizon using its own evaluation returns. If every horizon is negative after credible costs, the tested tactic has no economic justification from this study.

Primary paired contrasts are beta vs reversal2, trend-gated vs reversal5, compression vs continuation5, blend vs each constituent, and selector vs blend: six contrasts at each hold/profile, 36 declared paired comparisons. Report ending equity, net/gross P&L, drawdown, fees, median entry notional, mean invested exposure, turnover, completed trades and win/loss expectancy; also weekly/monthly/yearly results and observed regime exposure. Zero-interest cash and same-interval whole-share SPY are references with different exposure and holding duration. Missing required prices invalidate full-account metrics rather than silently truncating the path. Account failures stay in the 54-attempt denominator.

Date-block uncertainty is exploratory if added, because all dates have already been exposed to prior research and the comparisons are dependent. None is a genuinely untouched holdout. A candidate for later qualification must show positive net economics, stability across years/regimes and drawdown compatible with the owner's 30% preference; these necessary checks do not prove future profitability or enforce a guaranteed loss cap.

Academic evidence is motivation for hypotheses, not evidence that this cash-only next-close implementation is profitable. Short-term reversal can reflect liquidity provision and bid-ask effects; daily sampled closes may not capture achievable fills. [Cox and Peterson, Stock Returns following Large One-Day Declines](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1994.tb04428.x). Momentum losses can depend on market decline/rebound and volatility states, although much of the cited mechanism concerns long-short momentum rather than this long-only account. [Daniel and Moskowitz, Momentum Crashes](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439). Volatility can help forecast risk more than return; our binary rule selector is a distinct hypothesis, not a replication of inverse-variance monthly allocation. [Moreira and Muir, Volatility Managed Portfolios](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w22208/w22208.pdf).
